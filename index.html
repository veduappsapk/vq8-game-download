How to Use DTF Transfers

Using DTF is easier than learning screen printing. Here is a simple step-by-step guide.

Step 1: Get Your Design Ready

You need a digital image to print. Make sure your file has a transparent background. This stops a white box from printing around your design. Save your file as a PNG at 300 DPI for the best quality.

Step 2: Print on the Film

The shop uses a printer to put your design onto a special PET film. They lay down a white base first. This helps the colors show up bright, even on black shirts.

Step 3: Add the Adhesive Powder

While the ink is still wet, the worker shakes a glue powder over it. This powder is what makes the design stick to the fabric. The powder, also called hot melt powder, is key to DTF supplies in New York.

Step 4: Cure the Film

The film goes through a machine or oven to melt the powder. This dries the glue. It makes the design ready to transfer. At this stage, the design is on the film and ready to press.

Step 5: Heat Press the Shirt

This is the final step. You place the film onto your t-shirt. Then, you press it with a heat press machine. A typical press uses about 290°F for 12 seconds. After this, you peel off the film. Your design is now on the shirt.

📌 Pro Tip: Pre-press your garment to remove wrinkles, use the right pressure, and always check the peel method (cold peel vs hot peel) for best results.

Tips for Great Results

To make your shirts look professional, follow these tips. They come from expert printers in the city.

  • Pre-Press Your Garment: Always press the shirt for a few seconds before you apply the transfer. This removes wrinkles and moisture. It helps the ink stick better.
  • Use the Right Pressure: The heat press needs firm pressure. If the pressure is too light, the design might peel off.
  • Cold Peel vs. Hot Peel: Some films need to cool before you peel them. Others peel hot. The provider will tell you which type you have.
  • Wash with Care: Tell your customers to wait 24 hours before washing. They should wash the shirt inside out in cold water. This makes the print last longer. Avoid high heat drying for the best results.
